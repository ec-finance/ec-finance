Summary of Ira Sharenow's Finance pages

Mostly accurate factsThe pages' numbers are largely real.Most figures trace to audited reports. The history they document (negative reserves 2017–2020, going-concern audit opinions, a "high risk" State Auditor designation, annual short-term borrowing) did happen.
Framing choicesA few key charts and comparisons lean one way.The headline chart changes accounting basis in its final year. The spending-vs-inflation test starts at the 2021 pandemic low. The "$2.9M average" budget revision mixes bases and uses only the three most recent years.
OmissionThe recovery is documented unevenly.The credit rating page records a strong recovery, but the State Auditor page stops in 2021 and leaves out that the high-risk designation was removed in December 2024.
Real pressuresTwo long-term concerns hold up.The recovery was mostly revenue-driven: voter-approved taxes, federal relief and tax growth. Pensions are a genuine weight; El Cerrito's liability relative to revenue is 2nd highest of 426 California cities, and the City's own plan has unassigned reserves dipping to 16% in FY2027-28.
